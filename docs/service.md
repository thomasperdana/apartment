Here are the service providers and businesses that support an apartment development, grouped by when they're needed.

## Pre-development
- **Real estate brokers and land acquisition specialists:** find and negotiate sites
- **Market research and feasibility firms:** demand studies, rent comps, and absorption analysis
- **Land-use attorneys and zoning consultants:** rezoning, variances, and entitlement strategy
- **Surveyors, title companies, and escrow agents:** boundary surveys, title insurance, and closing
- **Environmental consultants:** Phase I/II assessments, wetlands, and endangered species reviews
- **Geotechnical engineers:** soil borings and foundation recommendations
- **Civil engineers:** grading, drainage, utilities, and site layout

## Design and approvals
- **Architects:** schematic design through construction documents
- **Structural, MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing), and fire protection engineers**
- **Landscape architects and interior designers**
- **Traffic engineers:** traffic impact studies and parking analysis
- **Expediters and permit consultants:** shepherd applications through the city
- **Code consultants:** accessibility (ADA/FHA) and energy code compliance
- **Community engagement or public relations firms:** neighborhood meetings and hearings, especially for contested projects

## Financing and capital
- **Construction lenders, banks, and debt funds**
- **Mezzanine and preferred equity providers**
- **Equity investors, family offices, and syndication platforms**
- **Permanent lenders:** Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, HUD, and life insurance companies
- **Tax credit and bond advisors:** LIHTC syndicators and tax-exempt bond consultants, if affordable housing is involved
- **Government incentive programs:** grants, tax abatements, and TIF districts
- **Financial advisors and accountants:** budgeting, cost segregation studies, and tax structuring

## Construction
- **General contractors and construction managers**
- **Subcontractors:** framing, concrete, roofing, MEP, drywall, elevators, and more
- **Equipment and material suppliers:** windows, appliances, cabinetry, and fixtures
- **Construction inspectors and testing labs:** special inspections and materials testing
- **Builder's risk and liability insurers, and surety bond providers**
- **Scheduling and cost-control software vendors**
- **Temporary utilities, fencing, and site services**

## Legal, insurance, and compliance
- **Construction attorneys:** contracts, lien issues, and disputes
- **Insurance brokers:** property, liability, and umbrella coverage
- **Title and survey firms:** for final closing and permanent financing
- **Compliance consultants:** fair housing, ADA audits, and local ordinances

## Leasing and operations
- **Property management companies:** day-to-day operations, which many owners outsource
- **Leasing agents and marketing firms:** listings, websites, signage, and lease-up campaigns
- **Resident screening services:** credit, background, and eviction checks
- **Maintenance and janitorial contractors**
- **Property management software providers:** Yardi, AppFolio, RealPage, and similar platforms
- **Utility billing and smart-home technology vendors**
- **Security and access-control providers**
- **Landscaping, snow removal, pest control, and waste management**

## Sale or refinancing (exit)
- **Commercial real estate brokers:** sale or investment marketing
- **Appraisers and valuation firms**
- **Refinance lenders and loan brokers**
- **Investment sales and capital markets advisors**

## Quick priorities
If you're just starting, the services you'll need first are a land-use attorney, a feasibility analyst, a civil engineer, an architect, and a lender or equity partner. Construction and property management come later, but you'll want to line them up before construction starts.
